# Consciousness & Philosophy of Mind

The mystery of subjective experience—the "what it is like" (qualia) to undergo a conscious state—remains one of the most stubborn explanatory chasms in science. Formulated by David Chalmers as the **Hard Problem**, it questions why physical information processing in the brain is accompanied by inner phenomenal experience at all.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The Hard Problem is distinct from the "easy problems" of cognitive function (discrimination of stimuli, verbal reportability, focus of attention), which fall cleanly under empirical [[Neuroscience & Synaptic Plasticity]] and computational [[Artificial Intelligence]].

## 1. Primary Theoretical Frameworks

### Integrated Information Theory (IIT)
Pioneered by Giulio Tononi, IIT posits that consciousness is a fundamental property of physical systems possessing high intrinsic cause-effect power.
- Quantified by the scalar metric $\Phi$ (Phi).
- Connects directly with [[Information Theory & Entropy]], arguing that phenomenal structure is maximally irreducible integrated information.

### Global Workspace Theory (GWT)
Developed by Bernard Baars and Stanislas Dehaene:
- Models conscious awareness as a centralized functional blackboard or "neuronal workspace" that broadcasts information globally across specialized modular processors.
- Correlates with the deliberate, serial executive processes detailed in [[Cognitive Architectures & Dual Process Theory]].

## 2. Physics, Brain, and Substrates

- **Quantum Mind Hypotheses**: Roger Penrose and Stuart Hameroff (Orch-OR) speculate that consciousness arises from quantum gravitational state reduction inside neuronal microtubules, linking subjective collapse to [[Quantum Decoherence & Measurement]].
- **Predictive Processing**: Anil Seth and Andy Clark conceptualize consciousness as a "controlled hallucination," driven by top-down error minimization via the [[Free Energy Principle & Active Inference]].
- **Cybernetic Boundaries**: Can a silicon-based neural network instantiate phenomenal feel, or is it merely simulating functional behavior? The debates in [[Cybernetics & Feedback Loops]] and [[Complex Systems & Emergence]] explore whether consciousness is an emergent substrate-independent macro-phenomenon.
