# Cybernetics & Feedback Loops

Originating from the Greek *kybernetes* ("steersman"), Cybernetics was established by Norbert Wiener in 1948 as the transdisciplinary study of **control, communication, and circular causal mechanisms** in machines, organisms, and social organizations.

## The Paradigm of Circular Causality

Classical Newtonian physics favored linear causality ($A \to B$). Cybernetics revolutionized scientific epistemology by demonstrating that complex behaviors are sustained by closed loops of circular causality:

```
  ┌───────────┐       Action       ┌───────────┐
  │ Controller│ ─────────────────> │ Controlled│
  │  (Agent)  │                    │  Process  │
  └─────▲─────┘                    └─────┬─────┘
        │          Sensory State         │
        └────────────────────────────────┘
                 Feedback Error
```

### Modes of Feedback
- **Negative Feedback (Degenerative / Attenuation)**: Counters discrepancies between actual and desired states; produces homeostasis, stabilization, and goal-directed teleological behavior (e.g., thermostats, glucose regulation, autopilot).
- **Positive Feedback (Generative / Amplification)**: Reinforces perturbations, leading to exponential runaway growth, morphological phase shifts, or catastrophic structural collapse, directly studied in [[Nonlinear Dynamics & Chaos Theory]].

## Foundational Theorems

### 1. The Good Regulator Theorem (Conant & Ashby, 1970)
> *"Every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system."*

This profound mathematical theorem proves that an optimal controller cannot merely react blindly to inputs—it must embody an internal generative simulation of its environment, prefiguring the core tenets of the [[Free Energy Principle & Active Inference]] and model-based [[Artificial Intelligence]].

### 2. Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety
> *"Only variety can destroy variety."*

For an agent to maintain homeostatic stability against environmental perturbations, the number of states its internal controller can adopt must be equal to or greater than the variety of external disturbances. This theorem is mathematically rooted in channel capacities from [[Information Theory & Entropy]].

## Legacy Across Modern Thought

- **Biological Homeostasis**: The nervous system operates as an intricate web of recursive chemical and electrical feedback circuits detailed in [[Neuroscience & Synaptic Plasticity]].
- **Second-Order Cybernetics**: Heinz von Foerster introduced the "cybernetics of observing systems," asserting that the observer cannot be cleanly decoupled from the observed, directly resonating with the measurement dilemmas of [[Quantum Decoherence & Measurement]] and [[Consciousness & Philosophy of Mind]].
- **Self-Organized Networks**: Circular causality is the primary driver of spontaneous pattern formation in [[Complex Systems & Emergence]].
